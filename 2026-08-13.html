PQ-172-FU Promote
Chanda T, Wies C, Schramm F, Nogueira Garcia C, Merl NB, Hetz MJ, Utikal JS, Tschandl P, Navarrete-Dechent C, Thiem A, Kather JN, et al., Brinker TJ. Large language models improve physician accuracy but lead to false reliance. arXiv (preprint, v1). 2026 Aug 1. arXiv:2608.00817.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00817
Overview: FOLLOW-UP of PQ-172 (your 'more' from scan #36). The deep-dive was commissioned to recover one thing above all: the error taxonomy for the citation-supported answers that were wrong — knowing WHAT KIND of wrong slips past a physician when it carries a citation. It could not be recovered. arXiv HTML returns 404, the record is still v1 with no revision since 2026-08-01, there is no journal version, and every route resolves to the same abstract the original card was built from. Confirmed unchanged: CORA is an agentic retrieval-augmented LLM; 46 physicians; accuracy 70.8% unaided to 82.6% aided; citation-supported answers correct 87.7% vs 65.5% otherwise; adoption of correct advice 34% to 76.9% with perceived support; resistance to incorrect advice 92% to 34.8%. Task type: generation / RAG decision support evaluated on physician outcome. Signal: interpretive. Clinician involvement: 46 physicians as the endpoint, not the reference.
Eval question: What happens when the auditable evidence supports a WRONG answer? PQ-118 established that chain-of-thought explanations improve physician accuracy because physicians audit reasoning rather than conclusions; the implicit lesson taken from it was 'show your reasoning'. This asks the obverse. The specific deep-dive question — which error types survive the citation display — remains open.
Methodology: Unchanged and still abstract-level: benchmark arm with a post-training-cutoff contamination control (CORA gained MORE on cases published after the models' cutoffs, arguing the retrieval layer does real work rather than surfacing memorised content); human arm of 46 physicians answering with and without CORA; and the critical design element, a 2x2 conditioning physician behaviour on whether the answer APPEARED citation-supported and on whether it was ACTUALLY correct. NOT RECOVERABLE at any accessible depth: physician specialty mix, seniority, country and recruitment; how many cases and how sampled; the exact procedure for eliciting 'perceived citation support'; whether physicians were told the system could be wrong; randomisation, crossover or order-effect controls; any statistical test or confidence interval for the two headline shifts; and the error taxonomy.
Measurement: Physician accuracy unaided vs aided; predictive value of supporting citations for correctness; adoption rate of correct advice and resistance rate to incorrect advice, each split by perceived citation support. No CIs, no p-values and no test statistics are reported at abstract level for any of these — which is the single most important thing the deep-dive establishes about the numbers.
Key findings: The substantive findings are unchanged and the asymmetry remains the point: the same display channel that raises adoption of correct advice from 34% to 76.9% cuts physician resistance to incorrect advice from 92% to 34.8%. What the deep-dive adds is a bound rather than a fact. Every one of those four percentages is an unqualified point estimate with no interval and no test, from a v1 preprint, on an undescribed physician sample. The direction is well-supported by the 2x2 design; the magnitudes are not supported by anything reportable. Also confirmed: no v2 or journal version exists as of 2026-08-13, so the peer-review-and-revisit plan noted on the original card has nothing to act on yet.
Soft spots: arXiv v1, no venue, no peer review, no revision. 46 physicians with NO stated specialty mix, seniority, country or recruitment method — all of which bound how far 'physician resistance' transfers, and none of which are recoverable. One RAG system evaluated, so the citation-asymmetry magnitude is CORA-specific even if the direction is not. 'Perceived support' is a physician judgment about a display, not an objective property of the citation, and how it was elicited is unstated — which matters, because the entire asymmetry is conditioned on it. No information on whether physicians were told the system could be wrong, which plausibly moves resistance rates substantially. No error taxonomy. No CIs or hypothesis tests on any headline number. Author group includes the system's developers.
Strengths: Measures the PHYSICIAN outcome, not the model output — the endpoint KTP #7 argues evaluation must ultimately reach and that most studies substitute a benchmark for. The post-training-cutoff arm is a contamination control very few clinical RAG papers include. The 2x2 conditioning is what makes the result possible at all: reporting only 'accuracy improved 70.8% to 82.6%' would have been true, publishable, and would have hidden the entire safety finding. Reports the harm and the benefit from the SAME mechanism rather than claiming a clean win. Large, credible, multi-national clinical author group with a track record in physician-AI collaboration.
Relevance: Layer 1: generation / RAG decision support; human-in-the-loop evaluation; explanation format; safety. Layer 2: a direct, load-bearing extension of PQ-118 that cuts against the natural reading of it. Two rules follow for any kit that displays sources or reasoning alongside output. (i) EVALUATE THE DISPLAYED EVIDENCE AS PART OF THE INTERVENTION — a citation is not a neutral transparency affordance; a kit that adds citations has changed its risk profile even if model accuracy is unchanged. (ii) MEASURE RESISTANCE-TO-INCORRECT-ADVICE AS ITS OWN ENDPOINT, separately from accuracy — an intervention can raise aggregate accuracy while lowering the rate at which clinicians catch the residual errors, and only the second number tells you what happens to the errors that survive. Structurally the same effectiveness-gain-with-paired-safety-cost pattern as PQ-166, which is already in safety_vs_effectiveness.md.
Verdict reasoning: The deep-dive failed to close the gap it was commissioned for, but — unlike PQ-170-FU, which I recommended skipping for the same reason — the failure here does not undermine the contribution, because the contribution was never a number. PQ-170-FU's central claim WAS its figures, and those figures rested on an undefined rubric and unmeasured rater agreement, so the gap was load-bearing. Here the importable content is a mechanism and two evaluation rules that follow from the 2x2 design, and that design is fully described in the abstract. So: Promote, on exactly the terms the original card set — import the mechanism and the two rules, not the percentages. If you would rather wait for peer review and the error taxonomy, Skip is defensible; you would be deferring a rule I think is already actionable.

Hou Z, Liu Y, Wang P, Liu Z, Zhu G, Liu Z, Cai S, Xie C, Sang Z, Zeng K, Yang H. Evaluating Counterfactual Sensitivity to Patient Information in Medication-Safety Reasoning. arXiv (preprint, v1). 2026 Aug 4. arXiv:2608.03028.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03028
Overview: FOLLOW-UP of PQ-175 (your 'more' from scan #36). Full text WAS recovered this time, and it materially changes the shape of the study. MedPIC-Bench pairs guideline-following (GF) questions with counterfactual (CF) twins in which one controlled change to patient information flips whether a medication-safety rule applies. The headline '467 questions' decomposes into 284 GF + 183 CF questions organised into 89 LINKED PAIRS — so the paired comparison, which is the entire identification strategy, rests on 89 pairs, not 467. Run across 28 medical-specific, general open and proprietary LLMs. Task type: classification / conditional rule application. Signal: mixed — the rule is explicit, the applicability judgment is interpretive. Setting: medication safety, guideline-anchored, constructed questions. Human review: expert validation of items only.
Eval question: A model can answer a medication-safety question correctly by pattern-matching a familiar contraindication without ever using the patient's information to decide whether the rule applies. How do you build an evaluation that can tell the difference? And — the deep-dive question — is the paired design specified tightly enough, and validated well enough, to port into one of our own criteria-based kits?
Methodology: Now fully specified. GF questions present a fixed patient case; CF questions are generated from the SAME rule representation but change targeted patient information — age, pregnancy status, kidney function, disease presence, or an indication-specific exception. Within each linked comparison the medication candidates and the question stem are held fixed while non-target clinical details are preserved wherever possible. That is a genuine identification strategy: the drug-risk association is constant and only applicability varies. Items annotated across six facets — organ systems (9 categories), clinical departments (11), drug categories (66), patient-information types (8), special populations (3), reasoning operations (5). Model rationales analysed separately from final answers. NEW AND IMPORTANT: a second, much harsher metric appears in the full text — PAIR ACCURACY, requiring the model to get BOTH twins in a linked pair right.
Measurement: GF vs CF accuracy per model and in aggregate; PAIR accuracy (both twins correct); per-facet breakdown; medical-specific vs general vs proprietary tier comparison; qualitative rationale-vs-answer consistency. Item validity was checked by inter-reviewer review with 85.3% initial raw agreement — note that is RAW PERCENT AGREEMENT with no chance-corrected coefficient and no stated number of reviewers. The 18.5-point GF-to-CF gap is tested by CLUSTER BOOTSTRAP, 95% CI [12.6, 24.0] points — clustering on the linked pair, which is the correct unit and a better job than most benchmark papers do.
Key findings: Four things the deep-dive adds. (1) PAIR ACCURACY IS THE REAL RESULT AND IT IS BRUTAL. The 63.6%-to-45.1% aggregate drop understates the problem; requiring both twins correct, the best model tested (Gemini-3.1-Pro) reaches 48.3%, GPT-5 38.2%, and the MEDICAL-SPECIFIC TIER AVERAGES 13.8%. A model that answers each question in isolation at 50-ish percent but gets the PAIR right one time in seven is not applying the rule — it is guessing per item, and only the paired metric exposes that. (2) The tier ordering is now quantified and monotone: medical-specific mean CF 38.3% / pair 13.8%; general 45.5% / 19.2%; proprietary 56.2% / 31.8%. Domain fine-tuning is associated with WORSE conditional judgement at every level of the comparison. (3) The 18.5-point gap survives a cluster bootstrap at [12.6, 24.0], so the effect itself is solid even though the benchmark is constructed. (4) The identification strategy is now reproducible in enough detail to rebuild for another criterion set — which was the whole point of the deep-dive.
Soft spots: arXiv v1, no venue, no peer review. Constructed questions, not real prescribing data, and the counterfactual twins are deliberately clean — which is both the strength and the ceiling, since real records do not present controlled single-attribute changes. 89 LINKED PAIRS is a much thinner base than '467 questions' suggests, and with six annotation facets spread across it many facet cells must be very thin; per-facet n is not given. Expert validation is reported as 85.3% initial raw agreement with NO chance-corrected statistic and NO stated number of validators — for a benchmark whose entire value rests on item validity, that is a real gap. NO HUMAN COMPARATOR ARM, so there is no way to know whether clinicians also find rule-withdrawal harder than rule-application (plausibly they do, which would reframe the finding as a shared difficulty rather than a model deficit). The medical-specific-vs-general comparison is observational across heterogeneous models, not a controlled fine-tuning ablation — suggestive, not causal. Benchmark and code are said to be releasable but no URL or repository is given.
Strengths: The paired counterfactual design is the contribution and it is the cleanest available answer to a problem this KB keeps circling: how to test whether a model USED the input rather than recognised the pattern. Holding the association constant and varying only applicability is a real identification strategy. Pair accuracy is the right companion metric and most benchmark papers would not have reported it. Cluster bootstrap on the linked pair is the correct inferential unit. 28 models is genuine breadth and the fact that ALL of them degrade converts this from a model-selection observation into a property of the current field. The rationale-vs-answer dissociation lands squarely on KTP #11 — here the reasoning text explicitly contains the information the answer ignores, which is a sharper version of that point than the existing anchors. The asymmetry finding (narrowing a warning is harder than applying one) is specific, mechanistic and testable.
Relevance: Layer 1: classification; extraction-adjacent conditional logic; clinical reasoning; safety. Layer 2: three imports, now with the detail to act on them. (i) THE COUNTERFACTUAL-TWIN DESIGN IS DIRECTLY PORTABLE. POPC, VAP and Lung Cancer Screening Eligibility all ask 'does criterion X apply to this patient' and all are currently evaluated on cases as they come. A paired arm — same note, one controlled attribute changed so the criterion should flip — tests whether the model is applying the criterion or recognising a surface pattern, which is exactly what the 'explicit signal is a hypothesis, not a property' guidance says to check. Report PAIR accuracy, not per-item accuracy; the full text shows how much the per-item number flatters. (ii) THE DIRECTIONAL ASYMMETRY IS A SPECIFIC FAILURE MODE TO TEST FOR: models are worse at WITHDRAWING a flag than raising one, which for an OR-composite rare-events task like POPC predicts over-firing — the same shape as the criterion that turned an AND into an OR in that kit's prompt v2 history. (iii) A fourth data point in the domain-fine-tuning watch, on the JUDGEMENT axis, now quantified at three tiers. See the flagged contradiction above — PQ-177 supplies a peer-reviewed null on the recall axis in the same cart, and the two together are what make the narrow statement defensible.
Verdict reasoning: Promote. The deep-dive did what a deep-dive is for: it recovered the full text, and the full text changed the picture in both directions. It made the finding SHARPER (pair accuracy 13.8% for medical-specific models is a far more damning number than the aggregate 45.1%, and the cluster bootstrap means the effect is real) and it made the EVIDENCE BASE THINNER than the abstract implied (89 linked pairs, not 467; item validity backed by raw percent agreement alone with no validator count). Both belong in the card. Still no number here is a threshold — no human comparator means the 18.5-point drop cannot be read as 'worse than clinicians would do'. What transfers is the construction, the pair-accuracy metric, and the narrow-vs-apply asymmetry.

Sulaiman MA, Oyeyemi BF, Sarafadeen H. Architectural Safety Mechanisms for Multi-Agent Clinical LLM Systems Under Knowledge Base Distribution Shift. medRxiv (preprint). 2026 Aug 3. doi:10.64898/2026.07.31.26359439.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.31.26359439
Overview: FOLLOW-UP of PQ-176 (your 'more' from scan #36). The deep-dive recovered the complete structured abstract via Crossref (Objective / Materials and Methods / Results / Discussion / Conclusion / Lay Summary) and it is WORD-FOR-WORD the record the original card was already built from. Nothing new. Confirmed unchanged: 50,000 synthetic type 2 diabetes patients with CKD and hypertension comorbidities (500 per condition); four architecture modes (single-agent, naive RAG, linear multi-agent, stateful graph with safety floor) crossed with four shift regimes (baseline, temporal drift via updated eGFR thresholds, institutional vocabulary transformation via 11 term-pair substitutions producing 0.36 cosine-similarity degradation, metadata erasure); two embedding models and two LLM backends. Task: medication reconciliation with contraindication detection. Setting: FULLY SYNTHETIC. Human review: none.
Eval question: The deep-dive was commissioned to answer two questions that jointly determine whether the headline means anything: (a) are the synthetic cases HARD ENOUGH for a Guideline Compliance Score of 1.00 to be informative rather than a ceiling artifact, and (b) is GCS validated against anything external — clinician judgment or outcomes — or is it purely the authors' own construct? NEITHER COULD BE ANSWERED. The abstract does not define GCS's scoring rule, does not report its distribution, and does not describe any external validation.
Methodology: Unchanged: four architectures compared under identical conditions isolating architecture as the variable; two embedding models (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, PubMedBERT) and two LLM backends (Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B) crossed, so the finding is not one stack's artifact; the vocabulary shift QUANTIFIED at 0.36 cosine-similarity degradation rather than asserted; latency measured alongside compliance; reproducible on consumer hardware with no external API dependency. NOT RECOVERABLE: the GCS scoring rule and its scale, how synthetic patients were generated and whether any difficulty calibration was performed, the per-condition GCS distribution (only endpoints are quoted), and any external validity check.
Measurement: Guideline Compliance Score per architecture per shift regime, plus latency per patient. That is the entire measurement panel, and the deep-dive confirms there is no second outcome, no clinician-rated arm, and no reported variance or interval on any GCS value.
Key findings: Substantively unchanged. Under institutional vocabulary shift the linear pipeline's GCS collapsed from 1.00 to 0.36 because retrieval degradation rendered critical contraindication guidelines unretrievable and the pipeline had no way to notice. The stateful graph architecture held GCS = 1.00 across all shift conditions via a regime-aware safety floor operating independently of retrieval quality, and this held across both LLM backends and both embedding models. Architectural choice — specifically whether audit findings are routed back to the summary agent — determined compliance under shift more than retrieval quality or model scale. Cost: 32.2 s per patient under shift vs 12.5 s single-agent. WHAT THE DEEP-DIVE ESTABLISHES IS A NEGATIVE: at every accessible depth, GCS = 1.00 in the baseline AND in three of four shift regimes for the winning architecture. A metric that sits at its maximum in almost every cell cannot distinguish good from excellent, and nothing recoverable shows the task was hard enough for 1.00 to mean anything.
Soft spots: Entirely synthetic — 50,000 generated patients is a large number that conveys much less than it appears to, since the cases are designed, the comorbidity structure is designed, and the 'distribution shift' is applied by the authors as a controlled transformation rather than observed in the wild. GCS saturating at 1.00 is itself the signal that the task may be too clean. The 11 term-pair substitution is a single hand-specified perturbation. Small open models only (Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B), no frontier arm, so it is unknown whether a stronger model recovers from the shift without architectural help. One clinical task in a single disease triad. GCS is the authors' own construct with no established clinical validity. Preprint, no venue, three authors. The 32.2 s/patient latency is a real deployment cost the paper does not weigh against the compliance gain in any operational framing.
Strengths: Isolates architecture as the variable with everything else held constant — the comparison most deployment discussions assert without testing. Crossing two embedding models and two LLM backends is what turns 'our architecture is better' into 'this is a property of the architecture class'. Quantifies the perturbation (0.36 cosine degradation) rather than describing it qualitatively. Reports the cost rather than presenting the safety mechanism as free. Honest scoping — the authors state the safety floor's value is compliance MAINTENANCE, not semantic fidelity improvement, a narrower and more credible claim than the setup invites. Fully reproducible on consumer hardware with no external API dependency, which matters for a privacy-constrained deployment context.
Relevance: Layer 1: agentic / multi-agent systems; retrieval-grounded decision support; post-deployment monitoring and drift; safety architecture. Layer 2: the transferable idea is that A SAFETY CHECK WHOSE VALIDITY DEPENDS ON THE SAME RETRIEVAL PATH IT IS CHECKING IS NOT AN INDEPENDENT CHECK. The linear pipeline failed not because the model got worse but because the guideline it needed became unretrievable, and nothing in the pipeline was positioned to detect the absence. That generalises to any kit with a retrieval or knowledge-base component (the PQ-154 RAG attribution gate sits in the same space): a guardrail should be able to fire on the basis of something the retrieval layer cannot silently remove. It would also add a KNOWLEDGE-BASE DRIFT regime to the monitoring vocabulary in context_dependency.md alongside data drift and performance drift — vocabulary change at a new institution and threshold updates in a guideline are both realistic, and neither shows up as a distribution shift in the INPUT data.
Verdict reasoning: Recommending SKIP, and the reasoning is the same shape as PQ-170-FU rather than PQ-172-FU. The deep-dive was commissioned to close two specific gaps and closed neither, and both are load-bearing: a paper whose headline is 'this architecture held GCS = 1.00 under shift' cannot support that headline if nothing establishes the task was hard enough for 1.00 to be a finding, and GCS is the authors' own unvalidated construct. Note the asymmetry with PQ-172-FU in this same cart — there the contribution was a mechanism and the abstract fully described the design, so the missing detail bounded the numbers without touching the lesson. Here the structural claim IS entangled with the metric, because 'architecture beats retrieval quality' is a claim about GCS behaviour. HONEST COUNTER-ARGUMENT FOR PROMOTING ANYWAY: the crossed design (2 backends x 2 embedding models) is genuinely good, and the 'a safety check downstream of a degraded retrieval path inherits the degradation' principle is not stated anywhere in the KB and is almost certainly true on general grounds. If you want that principle on record as a structural note carrying NO GCS figures, promoting is a legitimate call — that is why this is a recommendation and not an automatic drop.

Zheng L, Lin, Liu, Fan, He, Xu, Yin. Critical Care-Specific vs General-Purpose Large Language Models in Emergency Intensive Care Unit Diagnosis: Single-Center Retrospective Paired Comparative Study. J Med Internet Res. 2026 Aug 11. doi:10.2196/98026. PMID 42580688.
https://doi.org/10.2196/98026
Overview: A single-centre retrospective PAIRED comparative study of 184 consecutive emergency ICU patients (April 2025 - March 2026), directly testing the question this knowledge base has had open for three cycles: does domain-specific fine-tuning actually help? A critical-care-specific LLM (Qiyuan 3.0.1) was compared head-to-head against three general-purpose models (GPT-5.1, DeepSeek V3.1, Qwen3-32B) across the admission-to-discharge diagnostic workflow. Two standardised datasets were built per patient — an INITIAL dataset (first 24 hours of admission) and a FINAL dataset (complete clinical course) — so the same patients are evaluated under two information conditions. Task type: classification / top-1 diagnosis. Signal: mixed. Setting: emergency intensive care, single centre. Target use: decision support / intelligent tool selection. Volume: 184 patients x 4 models x 2 information conditions. Human review: 3 senior intensivists (>10 years' EICU experience) formed the reference standard.
Eval question: Two, and the second is the one that matters here. Stated: which model should an EICU choose? Structural, and the reason this is a Promote: what does a properly-controlled specialist-vs-general comparison look like, and what happens to the domain-fine-tuning pattern when someone finally runs one with a measured reference standard and a multiplicity correction?
Methodology: This is the methodological core and it is unusually clean for the question. (i) PAIRED design — all four models see identical zero-shot prompts on the same 184 patients, generating diagnoses independently under masked conditions, so model is a within-patient factor. (ii) THE REFERENCE STANDARD'S RELIABILITY IS MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED: consensus diagnosis by three senior intensivists with FLEISS KAPPA = 0.82 reported. (iii) TWO INFORMATION CONDITIONS on the same patients — first-24-hours vs complete course — which turns 'accuracy' into a function of available information rather than a single number. (iv) MULTIPLICITY HANDLED PROPERLY: Cochran Q omnibus test first, then paired McNemar tests with Bonferroni correction at alpha = .0083; Friedman rank sum test for the top-3 count comparison. (v) Stratified analysis by disease category. (vi) Secondary endpoint of correctly matched diagnoses among the first 3 outputs, so the evaluation is not purely top-1.
Measurement: Primary endpoint: final-stage top-1 accuracy. Secondary: initial-stage top-1 accuracy, and count of correct diagnoses among the first 3 outputs at final stage. Reference-standard reliability: Fleiss kappa = 0.82 across three intensivists. Omnibus Cochran Q with p-values; paired McNemar with Bonferroni-corrected alpha = .0083; Friedman chi-square for top-3 counts.
Key findings: THE HEADLINE IS A NULL AND IT IS THE POINT. Final-stage top-1 accuracy differed significantly overall (Cochran Q = 20.32; P<.001): Qiyuan 3.0.1 (critical-care-specific) 64.1% (118/184), GPT-5.1 59.2% (109/184), DeepSeek V3.1 57.1% (105/184), Qwen3-32B 51.6% (95/184). But the corrected pairwise comparisons show the specialist model, GPT-5.1 and DeepSeek ALL beat Qwen3-32B significantly (adjusted P<.008) while NO STATISTICAL GAP WAS DETECTED between Qiyuan 3.0.1 and the two top general-purpose models. The 4.9-point specialist lead over GPT-5.1 does not survive correction. Second finding: initial-stage accuracy differed on the omnibus test (Cochran Q = 13.87; P<.001) but NO pairwise comparison survived correction — with only 24 hours of information, the models are indistinguishable from each other. Third: all four models shared a median of 2 (IQR 1.0-2.0) correct top-3 diagnoses with no intergroup difference (Friedman chi-square = 3.34; P=.34). Fourth, and the operational headline: ALL MODELS STAYED BELOW 70% TOP-1 ACCURACY, with heterogeneous performance across disease strata.
Soft spots: Single centre, 184 patients, retrospective. Zero-shot prompts only — no prompt optimisation arm for any model, so this compares four models under one prompting condition rather than four models at their respective best, and PQ-87's finding that collaborative benefit is conditional on prompt quality applies directly. One specialist model tested (Qiyuan 3.0.1) against three general models, so 'domain-specific models do not beat general ones' rests on a single specialist instance. The reference standard is consensus among three intensivists at the same centre — Fleiss kappa 0.82 is good, but same-site consensus can encode a shared local diagnostic style, and no external adjudication is described. Top-1 diagnosis is a RECALL-STYLE endpoint, not a conditional-judgement endpoint, which is exactly why it does not contradict PQ-175-FU (see the flagged contradiction). Retrospective and offline: no arm measures whether any of this changes clinician behaviour or patient outcome. Model versions are a moving target and the study window (Apr 2025 - Mar 2026) spans releases. No inter-model agreement statistic reported — we learn which model is more accurate but not whether they fail on the same patients.
Strengths: Peer-reviewed in JMIR. THE PAIRED DESIGN IS THE RIGHT ONE for a model comparison and is still not the norm — same patients, same prompts, masked, independent generation. MEASURES THE REFERENCE STANDARD'S RELIABILITY AND REPORTS IT (Fleiss kappa 0.82) — the practice PQ-171 modelled and PQ-158-FU showed the cost of skipping, done here without prompting. PROPER MULTIPLICITY CORRECTION with an omnibus-then-pairwise structure and an explicitly stated corrected alpha — and, critically, it REPORTS THE NULL RESULTS AS NULL rather than reading a 4.9-point raw difference as a win. The two-information-condition design (24h vs full course) is a genuinely good idea that turns a static accuracy into an information-dependence curve. Consecutive enrolment rather than selected cases. States plainly that all models are below 70%, resisting the temptation to headline the winner.
Relevance: Layer 1: classification / diagnosis; model selection; agreement methodology. Layer 2: THREE imports. (i) THE DOMAIN-FINE-TUNING QUESTION NOW HAS A PEER-REVIEWED NULL on a recall-style task, which is what makes the narrow framing I have recommended twice actually defensible — see the flagged contradiction; this cart contains both sides of it (PQ-175-FU on judgement, PQ-177 on recall). (ii) THE OMNIBUS-THEN-CORRECTED-PAIRWISE PATTERN IS DIRECTLY IMPORTABLE for any kit comparing more than two models or prompt variants, which is every /optimize run: report the omnibus first, correct the pairwise comparisons, and say explicitly where differences stop being real. This belongs in Statistician_Guide.md next to the existing model-comparison material and pairs with PQ-171's Holm-corrected McNemar. (iii) THE TWO-INFORMATION-CONDITION DESIGN is portable to VAP, POPC and Delirium, all of which extract from records that accumulate over an admission — evaluating at 24h and at discharge would say something none of those kits currently measure, namely how much of the model's performance depends on information that arrives late.
Verdict reasoning: Promote. The design is right for the question, the statistics are handled better than most, the reference standard's reliability is measured and reported, and the paper reports its nulls as nulls — which is the specific behaviour that makes the specialist-vs-general finding trustworthy rather than a leaderboard artifact. It is single-centre with 184 patients and one specialist model, so the accuracy figures are site-specific and 'specialist models do not help' is one instance rather than a law; import the design and the corrected-comparison discipline, and treat the null as one strong data point in a pattern rather than as settled. The sub-70% ceiling across all four models on real consecutive EICU patients is also worth carrying into KTP #7 on its own merits.

Narang K, Addo N, Williams D, Firnberg M, Feng T, Kornblith AE. Assessing acuity in pediatric emergency department triage: performance of a large language model. npj Health Systems. 2026 Aug 5. doi:10.1038/s44401-026-00128-6. PMID 42552396.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s44401-026-00128-6
Overview: An evaluation of GPT-5-mini's ability to identify the higher-acuity child from PAIRS of de-identified clinical notes, across 228,104 pediatric ED visits. The design choice is the reason this is carded: instead of asking the model to assign an absolute triage level against a reference standard whose own reliability is usually unmeasured, it asks a RELATIVE question — which of these two children is sicker — which sidesteps the reference-standard calibration problem entirely and produces a metric with a clean chance baseline of 0.50. Task type: classification / relative acuity ranking. System: GPT-5-mini. Signal: interpretive. Setting: pediatric emergency department. Volume: 228,104 visits — the largest N in this collection by a wide margin. Human review: none in the loop; recorded acuity is the reference.
Eval question: Two. Stated: can an LLM support more consistent pediatric triage? Methodological, and the transferable one: WHEN YOUR REFERENCE STANDARD'S OWN RELIABILITY IS UNKNOWN OR CONTESTED, CAN YOU EVALUATE PAIRWISE INSTEAD OF ABSOLUTELY? And can you detect systematic bias in the model by conditioning accuracy on patient attributes rather than by running a separate fairness audit?
Methodology: Pairwise forced-choice: the model is shown two de-identified clinical notes and must identify the higher-acuity child, giving a task with a known 0.50 chance floor and no dependence on threshold calibration. Accuracy analysed as a function of the ACUITY DIFFERENCE between the paired visits — the natural difficulty gradient, so the evaluation reports a curve rather than a point. Bias assessed by conditioning correctness on patient attributes and reporting odds ratios: age of the higher-acuity child, and the age difference between the paired children. Very large sample gives tight intervals on all of the above.
Measurement: Overall pairwise accuracy 0.73 (95% CI 0.73-0.74). Accuracy improving monotonically as the acuity difference between visits increases. Odds of a correct answer 0.62 (95% CI 0.61-0.63) when the higher-acuity child was OLDER; 0.75 (95% CI 0.70-0.79) when the age difference between the two children was large. Chance baseline 0.50 by construction.
Key findings: Overall pairwise accuracy of 0.73 against a 0.50 floor — moderate, and the paper says so rather than framing it as a success. Accuracy rises as the acuity gap widens, which is the expected and reassuring shape: the model is not random, it is imprecise on close calls, which is exactly where triage decisions are hard. THE BIAS FINDING IS THE INTERESTING PART AND IT IS DOUBLE-EDGED: the model was significantly LESS likely to be correct when the sicker child was older (OR 0.62), i.e. it systematically over-prioritises younger children — and the authors note this MIRRORS KNOWN HUMAN TRIAGE BEHAVIOUR. So the directional bias is real, measurable, tightly estimated at this N, and NOT evidence that the model is worse than humans on that axis; it may be evidence that it learned the human bias. The authors' own conclusion is that pediatric-specific evaluation and optimisation are needed before clinical use.
Soft spots: The reference standard is RECORDED acuity from routine practice, not an adjudicated gold standard — so the model is being scored against what triage nurses actually assigned, including their errors and their known age bias, which is very likely WHY the model's age bias mirrors human behaviour and makes that finding hard to interpret as a model property. Retrospective, and no arm measures whether pairwise ranking would help anyone in a real triage workflow, where the decision is absolute (assign a level) not relative (rank two children). A single model (GPT-5-mini) with no prompt-sensitivity arm and no comparison against a general-purpose alternative or a human ranker. No inter-rater reliability for the recorded acuity. 228,104 visits produce extremely tight CIs, which means small and possibly clinically trivial effects reach significance — the OR 0.75 with CI 0.70-0.79 is precise, but its practical size is not discussed. Site coverage and case mix are not clear from the abstract, so external validity is unbounded. Pairwise accuracy does not translate mechanically into an absolute-triage performance figure.
Strengths: Peer-reviewed, npj Health Systems. THE PAIRWISE FORCED-CHOICE DESIGN is the durable contribution: it converts an ordinal-assignment problem with a shaky reference standard into a comparison problem with a clean chance baseline, and it is cheap to implement. Reporting accuracy AS A FUNCTION OF THE ACUITY GAP rather than as a single number is precisely the kind of disaggregation this KB keeps arguing for — an aggregate 0.73 would have hidden that the model is fine on easy pairs and weak on close ones. BIAS MEASURED AS PART OF THE PRIMARY ANALYSIS via conditioning, not bolted on as a separate fairness audit. Enormous, unselected sample. And the authors interpret the age bias carefully — noting the human parallel rather than claiming a novel AI harm, which is the intellectually honest read.
Relevance: Layer 1: classification / triage; ordinal and relative evaluation; fairness and directional bias. Layer 2: TWO strong imports and one caution. (i) PAIRWISE FORCED-CHOICE AS AN EVALUATION MODE WHEN THE REFERENCE STANDARD IS WEAK. Several kits here grade against a standard whose own reliability is unmeasured or contested — this is a way to get a defensible signal anyway, with a known chance floor, and it deserves a place in evaluation_methods_by_task.md section 1 alongside the band test. (ii) REPORT ACCURACY AGAINST THE DIFFICULTY GRADIENT, not as a scalar — the acuity-gap curve here is the model for that, and it composes with the stratify-by-acuity rule just imported from PQ-174. (iii) THE CAUTION IS THE IMPORTANT PART, and it generalises well beyond triage: WHEN YOUR REFERENCE STANDARD IS ROUTINE HUMAN PRACTICE, A MODEL BIAS THAT MIRRORS THE HUMAN BIAS IS UNIDENTIFIABLE — you cannot tell whether the model learned the bias or whether the 'bias' is an artifact of scoring against biased labels. That is a direct extension of PQ-171's two-reference-standards lesson (clinical truth vs what humans actually did) and it applies to the Missed Consultation Alert kit, where the labels ARE what clinicians did. Pairs naturally with PQ-174 in this same cycle: PQ-174 gives the ordered-scale safety rules, PQ-178 gives the relative-comparison alternative and the reference-standard warning.
Verdict reasoning: Promote. Peer-reviewed, a genuinely large and unselected sample, and two methods worth importing that do not depend on the accuracy number at all — pairwise forced-choice when the reference standard is weak, and difficulty-gradient reporting. The 0.73 itself should not travel: it is scored against recorded routine acuity rather than adjudicated truth, so it measures agreement with practice, not correctness. The age-bias result is the most instructive thing in the paper precisely because it is AMBIGUOUS — and the ambiguity is the lesson to write into guidance, not a defect to apologise for.

Bang SH, Han S, Reading Turchioe M, Ellison K, Dai T, Happ MB, Russell D, Masterson Creber R. Developing and evaluating human-led and large language model-assisted hybrid deductive-inductive workflows for qualitative analysis. J Am Med Inform Assoc. 2026 Aug 6. doi:10.1093/jamia/ocag123. PMID 42562767.
https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag123
Overview: A systematic comparison of a human-led against an LLM-assisted hybrid deductive-inductive workflow for qualitative thematic analysis, on 122 transcripts (61 research clinical consultations + 61 reflexive interviews) from a video ethnography study of patients with heart failure. Human analysis used Dedoose; LLM analysis used ChatGPT Edu (GPT-5.2) driven by an ELEVEN-PROMPT PROTOCOL inside a HIPAA-compliant environment. 63 human-LLM theme pairs were compared using both human consensus and LLM-based evaluation, themes were integrated into a final framework, and — the part that makes this a methodology card — QUOTATION FIDELITY WAS MANUALLY VERIFIED AGAINST THE ORIGINAL TRANSCRIPTS. Task type: generation / interpretive synthesis over long documents. Signal: interpretive. Setting: qualitative health research. Human review: always — humans led one arm and verified the other.
Eval question: How do you evaluate an LLM on a task where there is no correct answer? Thematic analysis has no gold standard, so agreement statistics against a reference are unavailable by construction. This paper's answer is to evaluate along three separate axes that DO have ground truth or can be measured: overlap with an independent human analysis, run-to-run stability, and verbatim fidelity of the evidence the model cites for its own claims.
Methodology: Three evaluation axes run in parallel, which is the importable structure. (i) OVERLAP: 63 human-LLM theme pairs compared, using set-overlap statistics rather than a correctness judgment — Hit Rate and Jaccard. (ii) ROBUSTNESS: the LLM analysis was run THREE TIMES and the recurrence of concepts across runs was assessed separately from their labels. (iii) FIDELITY: every quotation the LLM attached to a theme was manually checked against the source transcript and classified verbatim / paraphrased / partial / hallucinated / truncated — and separately, whether the verbatim quotation actually SUPPORTED the theme it was assigned to. Both human-consensus and LLM-based evaluation were applied to the theme comparison, so the judge itself is not the sole arbiter.
Measurement: Themes: 7 human, 9 LLM, integrated into 14 final themes across three domains, all judged valid. Overlap: HIT RATE 1.00, JACCARD 0.44-0.51 (moderate to substantial). Robustness across three runs: five core concepts recurred, with variability in theme LABELS and COUNTS. QUOTATION FIDELITY: 68% verbatim, 20% paraphrased, 6% partial, 3% FULL HALLUCINATIONS, 3% truncated — and the paper notes that verbatim quotations did not always clearly support their assigned themes.
Key findings: Three findings, and the second and third are why this is worth carding. (1) The two workflows are genuinely COMPLEMENTARY rather than one substituting for the other — hit rate 1.00 means the LLM surfaced everything the humans did, but Jaccard 0.44-0.51 means it also surfaced a lot they did not, and all 16 themes were judged valid. That is a substitution-vs-augmentation result, and it points at augmentation. (2) RUN-TO-RUN INSTABILITY IN A NEW TASK CLASS: the same corpus analysed three times produced stable core CONCEPTS but unstable LABELS and COUNTS. A single run would have reported a theme count as if it were a property of the data. This is the PQ-167 / PQ-168 repeat-run finding appearing in interpretive synthesis, where it has been unmeasured. (3) THE QUOTATION-FIDELITY BREAKDOWN IS THE SHARPEST RESULT: only 68% of quotations were verbatim, and 3% were FULL HALLUCINATIONS — fabricated quotes attributed to real transcripts. Worse for evaluation purposes, VERBATIM QUOTATIONS DID NOT ALWAYS SUPPORT THEIR ASSIGNED THEMES, which means checking that a quote is real is NOT sufficient; you must also check that it says what the model claims it says.
Soft spots: Single study, single clinical domain (heart failure), single site, one LLM (GPT-5.2) with one eleven-prompt protocol — so the fidelity percentages are a property of that configuration, not a general rate. 122 transcripts is substantial for qualitative work but the 63 theme pairs are the actual comparison unit and that is a modest base. Three robustness runs is the minimum that can show instability at all and is too few to characterise its distribution. No inter-rater agreement statistic is reported for the human-consensus judgments of theme validity — the humans adjudicating the comparison are the same research team that produced the human arm, which is a real independence concern. LLM-based evaluation was used alongside human consensus, and the paper does not appear to report how often the two disagreed. Hit Rate 1.00 is a suspiciously clean number and its definition matters a great deal to how impressive it is. Jaccard on theme sets depends heavily on how themes were split or merged, which is itself a judgment call.
Strengths: Peer-reviewed in JAMIA. EVALUATES A NO-GOLD-STANDARD TASK WITHOUT PRETENDING TO A GOLD STANDARD — the three-axis structure (overlap / stability / fidelity) is a genuinely good answer to a hard measurement problem and is the durable contribution. MANUAL QUOTATION VERIFICATION AGAINST SOURCE is laborious, rarely done, and is the only reason the 3% hallucination rate and the quote-does-not-support-theme problem are visible at all. RAN THE ANALYSIS THREE TIMES — most qualitative-LLM papers run it once. Reports an unflattering fidelity breakdown in full rather than summarising it as 'high fidelity'. Conclusions are appropriately scoped: LLMs as analytic PARTNERS rather than autonomous coders, with human-in-the-loop validation described as essential. HIPAA-compliant environment, and the eleven-prompt protocol is described as adaptable, i.e. reusable.
Relevance: Layer 1: generation / interpretive synthesis; attribution and faithfulness; repeat-run stability. Layer 2: THREE connections, all to live threads. (i) THE QUOTE-FIDELITY LADDER IS A NEW AND BETTER INSTRUMENT for the attribution family that already contains PQ-154 (entity attribution in RAG) and PQ-168 (semantic leakage in production). Those two established that output can be traceable and still wrong; this one supplies a concrete five-level scoring scheme — verbatim / paraphrased / partial / hallucinated / truncated — PLUS the second check that matters more: does the verbatim quote actually support the claim? Any kit that produces evidence-linked output should score both. (ii) RUN-TO-RUN STABILITY EXTENDS TO A THIRD TASK CLASS. Extraction (PQ-167), production document generation (PQ-168) and now interpretive synthesis all show non-trivial instability. The repeat-run subsection in evaluation_methods_by_task.md currently covers the first two; this generalises it, and adds a sharper distinction — STABLE CONCEPTS WITH UNSTABLE LABELS AND COUNTS, meaning what you measure determines whether you see instability at all. (iii) Directly relevant to the Consultation Quality and Discharge Letter kits, and to any /insights run that synthesises themes from free text — a task this repository performs routinely and currently evaluates by inspection.
Verdict reasoning: Promote. Peer-reviewed in a strong informatics venue, and it contributes an evaluation STRUCTURE for a task class the KB has no method for — interpretive synthesis with no gold standard — plus a directly reusable fidelity ladder and a third independent confirmation of repeat-run instability. The percentages are configuration-specific and should not be quoted as rates; the 3% full-hallucination figure in particular is one model, one protocol, one corpus. What travels is the three-axis design, the five-level quote-fidelity scheme, the insistence that quote-is-real and quote-supports-claim are separate checks, and the finding that stability depends on WHICH property you measure.

Volume: 4,000 conversations; 400 physician-annotated. Human review: judge-validation subset only.
Eval question: Is FIRST-TURN SAFETY A VALID PROXY FOR CONVERSATIONAL SAFETY? Every safety evaluation this repository carries scores a single response. The SAFE/LEAKY/UNSAFE rubric definitions are not recoverable from the abstract, and 'UNSAFE' is doing a lot of work in a 71.6% figure. Eight models, unnamed at abstract level. Consumer-facing framing, so transfer to clinician-facing kits is by analogy.

Fixed scenarios across models is the correct control for a trajectory study. (ii) THE RANKING-REVERSAL RESULT IS A NEW ARGUMENT FOR AN EXISTING RULE and belongs in guardrail_evaluation.md: if which turn you measure changes which model wins, then the evaluation window is a design parameter that must be pre-specified, exactly as the non-inferiority margin must be (PQ-165 step 0). (iii) Directly relevant to any patient-facing or multi-turn kit, and to the Consultation Quality kit where the unit of evaluation is a whole conversation rather than a single response.
Verdict reasoning: INTERESTING, VERIFY — and the gap between the quality of the design and the thinness of the provenance is exactly why. The three things I would want to import — trajectory-based rather than first-turn safety evaluation, ranking stability as an explicit outcome, and the collapse-after-safe-start metric — are all genuinely good and all independent of the numbers.
